Which Bay Area cities are under a flight path?

The published landing and departure routes of San Francisco (SFO), Oakland (OAK) and San Jose (SJC), drawn over the 50 cities and towns of the four counties around them. The routes come from the FAA's own procedure file and the shading from the federal aircraft noise map. The figures in the table are measured from those two.

  • 15 of 50cities have a published landing path overhead, below 3,000 ft
  • 17 of 50have a coded departure route overhead, within 19 miles of the runway
  • 26 of 50have at least 1% of their area on the federal aircraft noise map, from any airfield

How to read this

  • The lines are published routes, not radar tracks. Aircraft fly either side of them, and a city with no line can still hear aircraft.
  • This shows where the routes are, not how many aircraft use them. Which runways are in use depends on the wind, and the FAA's file does not say how often each is flown. The noise shading is the better guide to that.
  • Landing lines are each runway's final approach, from 3,000 ft down to the runway, at the glide angle the FAA publishes. "SFO 28L" means runway 28 Left at San Francisco. 4 runway ends have no published glide angle and are not drawn: SFO 01L, SFO 01R, OAK 15, OAK 33.
  • Departure lines are the coded departure procedures, drawn for their first 19 miles, or as far as their named waypoints go if that is less: where controllers take over and steer aircraft by radar, the line stops. Names such as GAPP7 are the FAA's own names for them.
  • Heights are above the runway, not above the ground. A city on a hill is closer to the aircraft than its figure says. Read each figure as give or take 100 ft.
  • Arrivals further out are not drawn. Before the final approach, aircraft descending towards an airport are steered by air traffic control, higher up and over a wide area. Cities under those arrival streams show no line here.
  • The noise shading is the aviation layer of the National Transportation Noise Map, 2022 edition, from the US Bureau of Transportation Statistics. It is a modelled 24-hour average, drawn over land only (which is why an approach over the bay has no shading until it reaches the shore), and it includes airfields this page draws no routes for, such as Livermore's and Hayward's own. The table names, for each city, the airfield inside its patch of shading; where water cuts a patch off from its airport, it is named for the airport whose approach runs through it, and where a patch holds two airfields, for the nearer. The Bureau publishes it to track trends and says it should not be used to evaluate noise at an individual location, so this page gives a share of a whole city and never a figure for an address.
  • City outlines are approximate. They are the Census Bureau's generalised boundaries, good to a few hundred feet, so treat anything right on a city line as approximate.
  • Cities only. The table lists incorporated cities and towns in San Francisco, San Mateo, Santa Clara and Alameda counties. San Francisco airport (SFO) is on unincorporated land, inside no city. Oakland airport (OAK) is inside the city of Oakland. San Jose airport (SJC) is inside the city of San Jose.
