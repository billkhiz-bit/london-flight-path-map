# N65 per postcode (planes louder than 65 dB a day)

Read before touching `scripts/n65_gate.py`, anything under `data/n65/`, or EASA's noise tables.

## What it is and why (2026-10-15 plan, 27-28 Oct groundwork)

N65 is how many planes a day pass louder than 65 dB LAmax: the CAA's own supplementary metric (ERCD reports, Tables
16-20) and what Battersea's "one plane every five minutes" means. Planes overhead counts what passes INSIDE a cone;
N65 counts what is LOUD, so it also answers the cone's knife edge (Hounslow TW3 3QL: 7 a day in its cell, 339 in the
next), where a postcode just off the path hears planes it is not under. Computed by ECAC Doc 29 (4th edition) from
our tracks and EASA's Aircraft Noise and Performance (ANP) data.

## THE PUBLISH RULE IS CODE, WRITTEN FIRST (2026-10-28)

`scripts/n65_gate.py` holds Bill's 27 Oct rule ("yes to both") as `publish_verdict(runs, database)`, N65's
`coverage_verdict()`, written before any model so the model is built to a fixed test. Heathrow against ERCD 2601
(annual average 16-hour day, 30 Mar 2025 - 29 Mar 2026): areas above 50/100/200 events within -10%/+15% at each
level, above 500 within +-30%, population inside the 50-event contour within +-20%. Gatwick against ERCD 2602 (summer,
16 Jun - 15 Sep 2025): -10%/+15% at every level (20 to 500). Under-reading gets the tight side. Like for like only
(the CAA's exact period, day and window). The deciding run is on EASA's FULL database; the free v2.3 tables only ever
build. Any miss or anything unmeasured: nothing is published. `tests/test_n65_gate.py` holds every edge both ways and
checks each target against the report's own text (CACI, not ONS, counts the CAA's population: hence +-20%).

  a contour's area by roughly 15-18%). **Per-type figures stay in those gitignored notes, never in this public file**
  (EASA's terms; the CLAUDE.md rule).

## The build, in order (~8-12 sessions; started 28 Oct)

1. DONE 28 Oct: sources kept in `data/n65/`; the gate and its tests.
2. DONE 28 Oct: `scripts/n65_doc29.py`, one flight's LAmax at one point by **Doc 29 5th edition** (ECAC, 19 May
   2026, in `source/doc29-5th/`; it replaces the 2016 4th; its wing-mounted installation coefficients are 0.0039 /
   0.062, not the 4th's 0.00384 / 0.0621). Held by `tests/test_n65_doc29.py` against **Volume 3's own worked
   results** (Part 1 workbook, sheet B-2: 3,516 segment-and-receptor rows, every intermediate value): geometry to
   5e-4 of a distance, corrections and table readings to 0.005 dB, judged in dB because ECAC's printed geometry
   carries 1-2 cm of unit-conversion rounding (worth <0.004 dB). Two bugs of mine it caught, both 3 dB: (a) the
   depression angle lost its SIGN (`acos(l/dp)`): on a descending segment seen from ahead the extension's closest
   point is underground and the angle is negative, read as zero (273 rows); (b) an observer ON the path's line (the
   runway centreline behind a take-off roll) got 90 degrees instead of ~0 (36 rows). LAmax takes distance, attenuation
   AND depression angle from the segment's NEAREST point (s4.5.5); the depression angle part is a reading where Doc
   29 is silent, which the CAA comparison will confirm or not. START-OF-ROLL directivity (4-22 to 4-25, behind each
   take-off ground-roll segment only) is in and matches Volume 3's own column on every row, once psi and d_SOR are
   measured on the GROUND as ECAC does (the source sits 1 m up: straight behind is exactly 180 degrees, -13.4791 dB;
   the slant distance gave 179.885 degrees, -13.4854). Line-of-sight blockage stays out (s4.5.8 advises against it
   by default); bank angle comes from the caller. Ten mutations each turned a test red, including the 4th edition's
   coefficients, a missing 762 m fade and the correction applied to landing rolls.
3. NEXT. Types: ADS-B `t` (ICAO designator) to ANP ids through EASA's substitution table (`phase0-out/`, 210
   codes, each with departure/approach dB deltas); engine variant by registration where it moves the answer (variants of
   one type can differ by several dB: the figures are in `phase0-out/`, not here). 189 codes, 21 with engine variants; every proxy is in ANP 2.3.
   Proposed for Bill: where the engine is unknown, the LOUDER variant, so a map that errs errs loud (the gate's

   default (up to 5% smaller contours for 5-10 times the computing).
