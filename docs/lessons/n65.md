# N65 per postcode (planes louder than 65 dB a day)

Read before touching `scripts/n65_gate.py`, `scripts/n65_profiles.py`, anything under `data/n65/`, or EASA's tables.

## What it is and why (2026-10-15 plan, 27-28 Oct groundwork)

N65 is how many planes a day pass louder than 65 dB LAmax: the CAA's own supplementary metric (ERCD reports, Tables
16-20) and what Battersea's "one plane every five minutes" means. Planes overhead counts what passes INSIDE a cone;
N65 counts what is LOUD, so it also answers the cone's knife edge (Hounslow TW3 3QL: 7 a day in its cell, 339 in the
next), where a postcode just off the path hears planes it is not under. Computed by ECAC Doc 29 (4th edition) from
our tracks and EASA's Aircraft Noise and Performance (ANP) data.

## THE PUBLISH RULE IS CODE, WRITTEN FIRST (2026-10-28)

`scripts/n65_gate.py` holds Bill's 27 Oct rule ("yes to both") as `publish_verdict(runs, database)`, N65's
`coverage_verdict()`, written before any model so the model is built to a fixed test. Heathrow against ERCD 2601
(annual average 16-hour day, 30 Mar 2025 - 29 Mar 2026): areas above 50/100/200 events within -10%/+15% at each
level, above 500 within +-30%, population inside the 50-event contour within +-20%. Gatwick against ERCD 2602 (summer,
16 Jun - 15 Sep 2025): -10%/+15% at every level (20 to 500). Under-reading gets the tight side. Like for like only
(the CAA's exact period, day and window). The deciding run is on EASA's FULL database; the free v2.3 tables only ever
build. Any miss or anything unmeasured: nothing is published. `tests/test_n65_gate.py` holds every edge both ways and
checks each target against the report's own text (CACI, not ONS, counts the CAA's population: hence +-20%).

## Step 5: one Heathrow day, end to end (built 3 Nov 2026, in an agent worktree; committed on `n65`, not pushed)

**What was built, in order (each module's docstring has the detail):**
- `scripts/n65_grid.py` (step 0, written 3 Nov earlier): n65_doc29's arithmetic over numpy arrays of observers, held
  equal to it to 1e-9 dB on every branch (11 tests; 6 of 7 mutations red, the 7th an equivalent mutant).
- `scripts/fetch_metar.py --air`: temperature, wind and altimeter per hour into `data/metar/<station>-<month>-air.csv`,
  a separate file, so the plane counter's QNH cache is never rewritten.
- `scripts/n65_tracks.py`: one movement's ADS-B points into the ground track it flew, s measured from the start of
  roll (departures) or the touchdown point (approaches), the bank from its own turns (B-14). 13 tests.
- `scripts/n65_path.py`: the profile cut by s3.6.2, s3.6.4 (held to Doc 29's own worked example: 304.8 m -> 17.2 m,
  37.8 m, ...) and s3.6.5, merged with the track (s3.6.6), close points dropped (s3.6.7); plus the step's choices
  (procedure, stage, headwind, touchdown). 17 tests. The two modules: 37 mutations, all red.
- `scripts/n65_day.py`: `day` runs one day (every movement the plane counter finds in 07:00-23:00 local, typed by
  `n65_types.resolve()`, flown for its hour's air on its own track, LAmax on a 100 m grid, N65 = flights reaching 65
  dB); `combine` averages day grids by weights. Each segment is evaluated only where its loudest possible level reaches
  the threshold: held EXACT against the unpruned sum, with and without the 0.5 dB margin (a test at the installation
  effect's 50 degree peak makes the bound bite). 36 tests; 16 of 18 mutations red, the two survivors (the start-of-roll
  term dropped from the bound) equivalent: at the reach's edge lateral attenuation takes ~10 dB (docstring).
  ~90 s a day on 4 workers.

**Readings made (each in its module; every one Bill's to confirm before a deciding run):**
(a) Procedure: ANP's DEFAULT; departures without one ICAO_B (NADP 2), then ICAO_A; approaches without one DEFAULT1. The
v9 Airbus types (A320neo family, A330neo, A350) have NO DEFAULT departure (ICAO_A/B only) and DEFAULT1/DEFAULT2
approaches, which differ only in how long they hold 3,000 ft far out; about a third of a day's movements take these
branches (32% on both days).
(b) Stage length: ADS-B carries no destination, so every departure is flown at its procedure's LIGHTEST and HEAVIEST
stage (by default weight; 'M', the MTOW, counts as heaviest) and the day is reported as that bracket. It matters: the
heaviest side's 50-event area is ~52% larger than the lightest's on both days.
(c) The day's air per movement: METAR temperature and QNH linear between the hourly reports, the wind of the nearest
report as a headwind along the runway; a calm wind 0; a variable wind (no direction) 0 kt, counted (249 movements on
8 Jul); no speed at all ANP's reference 8 kt, counted; no report within 2 h: not computed. Aerodrome elevation 83 ft
(EGLL AD 2.2), a per-airport table that refuses any other airport.
(d) Geometry: the ground track is the flight's ADS-B positions resampled every 200 m; the start of roll is the last
point at 15 kt or less on the centreline in the run that ends the take-off, else where a rolling start joined it, else
the landing threshold, said (6-24 a day); Heathrow's 09L/09R landing thresholds are displaced 309/308 m (AD 2.13), so
the fallback starts those rolls late. The touchdown is 50 ft / tan(glide) = 291 m past the threshold, read from ANP's
own approaches (they cross the threshold at 50 ft, 954 ft before touchdown). Track nodes are kept out of the take-off
roll (each roll segment carries its own start-of-roll directivity). Bank capped at 30 degrees, counted (1,170 and 1,962
track nodes on the two days). Beyond the observed track the path runs straight on 80 km (s3.6.6).
(e) Observers on a flat ground plane at the aerodrome's level (Doc 29's nominal plane; terrain not modelled), one
event per flight per cell (the loudest segment; Appendix F's second event left out), counted with no level spread
(Doc 29 s5.4's S ~ 2 dB distribution, 5-12, NOT applied: the CAA's report does not say whether ANCON applies it).
