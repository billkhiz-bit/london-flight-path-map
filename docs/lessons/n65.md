# N65 per postcode (planes louder than 65 dB a day)

Read before touching `scripts/n65_gate.py`, `scripts/n65_profiles.py`, anything under `data/n65/`, or EASA's tables.

## What it is and why (2026-10-15 plan, 27-28 Oct groundwork)

N65 is how many planes a day pass louder than 65 dB LAmax: the CAA's own supplementary metric (ERCD reports, Tables
16-20) and what Battersea's "one plane every five minutes" means. Planes overhead counts what passes INSIDE a cone;
N65 counts what is LOUD, so it also answers the cone's knife edge (Hounslow TW3 3QL: 7 a day in its cell, 339 in the
next), where a postcode just off the path hears planes it is not under. Computed by ECAC Doc 29 (4th edition) from
our tracks and EASA's Aircraft Noise and Performance (ANP) data.

## THE PUBLISH RULE IS CODE, WRITTEN FIRST (2026-10-28)

`scripts/n65_gate.py` holds Bill's 27 Oct rule ("yes to both") as `publish_verdict(runs, database)`, N65's
`coverage_verdict()`, written before any model so the model is built to a fixed test. Heathrow against ERCD 2601
(annual average 16-hour day, 30 Mar 2025 - 29 Mar 2026): areas above 50/100/200 events within -10%/+15% at each
level, above 500 within +-30%, population inside the 50-event contour within +-20%. Gatwick against ERCD 2602 (summer,
16 Jun - 15 Sep 2025): -10%/+15% at every level (20 to 500). Under-reading gets the tight side. Like for like only
(the CAA's exact period, day and window). The deciding run is on EASA's FULL database; the free v2.3 tables only ever
build. Any miss or anything unmeasured: nothing is published. `tests/test_n65_gate.py` holds every edge both ways and
checks each target against the report's own text (CACI, not ONS, counts the CAA's population: hence +-20%).

4. Thrust: ANP's default procedural profiles first; then thrust from each observed profile (Doc 29 Appendix B with the
   aerodynamic and jet engine coefficients) if the gate needs it. **First part BUILT 1 Nov** (committed on `n65`
   2 Nov, after a review closed three workbook readers left open), `scripts/n65_profiles.py`. It flies each ANP aircraft's default procedure the way **Doc 29
   5th edition Volume 2 Appendix B** does. Departures: take-off, constant-speed climb, accelerate by rate of climb or
   energy share, level, level-accelerate, the minimum-thrust cutback (B-13) and the 1,000 ft thrust transitions
   (B6.1.6). Approaches are built back from touchdown: descend, descend-decel, descend-idle, level, level-decel,
   level-idle, land, decelerate, with Case 1/Case 2 transitions, the headwind corrections (B-48, B-77) and the idle
   steps' non-ISA adjustments (B-57 to B-60, B-73, B-74). Jet thrust follows B-9 to B-11 (high-temperature
   coefficients, else the 30 C breakpoint on departures) and propeller thrust B-12. Take-off weight comes per stage
   length; the approach weight is 90% of the maximum landing weight. Where ANP has no steps, its fixed-point profile is
   returned as published. **For step 5:** `load_anp(v2.3, v9)` (a later release replaces an aircraft whole, as
   `n65_types` does), `profile(db, acft, op, profile_id, stage, Atmosphere(elevation, temp, QNH, headwind))` gives the
   points (distance from brake release, or to touchdown; height above the field; true airspeed; corrected net thrust per
   engine) and their source ('steps' or 'fixed'). `interpolate()` (3-2a to 3-2h) gives height, speed and power at any
   distance; `npd_power()` converts to the noise table's power parameter; `takeoff_roll()` splits the roll (s3.6.2).
   Units are Appendix B's (ft, kt, lb); convert to metres for `n65_doc29` at the boundary.
   **Verified against ECAC's own worked results:** Volume 3 Part 2 (gitignored workbook), all 26 reference cases. The 17
   departures cover both aircraft, ICAO A and B, energy share, deep cutback, the level variants, elevations to 5,000 ft,
   40 C, a QNH off standard, and headwinds from -5 to 8 kt. The 9 approaches cover descend, level-decel, level-idle,
   descend-decel and two descent angles, at headwinds 0 and 8. Every point's distance, height, true airspeed and thrust
   is held. Height, speed and thrust are held to 0.06: ECAC prints to 0.1, and the measured worst is 0.050. Distance is
   held to 0.2 ft: the measured worst is 0.145 ft in 50,000, from an Accelerate step's 1 ft stopping rule.
   `tests/test_n65_profiles.py` has 27 tests: 3 on ECAC's workbook (they skip without it); 22 on MADE-UP aircraft,
   hand-worked, run anywhere; a loader test on made-up files shaped like v2.3 and v9; and one that flies every ANP
   procedure (1,268 departures and 160 approaches with v9) on three days, shapes only (it skips without the tables).
   **28 mutations**, each breaking one rule, all turn it red. They include the B-22 headwind inverted, B-11 dropped, a
   500 ft transition, the transition keeping the old rating's thrust, iterating to 0.01 ft, rate of climb preferred to
   energy share, B-41 read literally, B-58 as printed, the idle adjustments dropped, the approach weight at 100%, the
   power interpolated linearly and a later release merged instead of replacing the aircraft.
   **Readings where Doc 29 is silent or slips** (each also said in the code):
   (a) ECAC's results need Table C-6.2's inputs AS PRINTED, to 0.1 ft and kt. The workbook carries the unrounded metric
   conversions, which move one case's last airborne point 0.5 ft and its level-decel thrusts up to 0.9 lb. The workbook
   also files the Level-Idle distance under Descent Angle (ANP's tables do not). The test rounds and refiles.
   (b) B-41 (Descend-Decel) writes ((TAS - w) / cos g). Read literally, ECAC's cases come out up to 9 lb low: the
   deceleration is too large by 1/cos^2. They are reproduced when its TAS is the HORIZONTAL airspeed:
   (TAS cos g - w) / cos g = TAS - w / cos g. With plain w, the 8 kt case is still 0.1 to 0.2 lb low.
   (c) B-21 gives a propeller's B-12 speed as the RMS of the end speeds; that reads every propeller climb up to 0.15%
   long (545 ft by 10,000 ft). ECAC's propeller cases need the true airspeed at the mid-step height.
   (d) B-58 adds 2 S_D Decel where B-57's sign needs a subtraction: as printed, ISA would not return the input speed.
   B-57 gives the wind in ft/s and B-58 in knots for the same quantity; ft/s is used.
   (e) B6.1.3: an Accelerate step ends at its final altitude estimate with the last iteration's distance. Iterating
   tighter, recomputing the distance, or taking the previous estimate each moves ECAC's cases by 10 to 22 ft, so ECAC's
   results carry the 1 ft rule.
   (f) B-18's acceleration needs knots in ft/s (k), which the text leaves out; the runway gradient defaults to 0.
   (g) For turboprops whose noise tables use "% of maximum static thrust" (their rows run 23 to 150), thrust in pounds
   is converted as 100 x CNT / static thrust. Doc 29 is silent; B-79 does the inverse.
   (h) Two v2.3 Level steps give no start speed: they hold the next point's speed. Where a step gives both rate of
   climb and energy share, energy share wins (B6.1.3, G3.6 footnote 42).
   (i) The idle adjustments use the next point's computed speed on the day, and the next step's inputs for the
   reference day. A Descend-Idle followed by any step Doc 29 does not name keeps its inputs.
   (j) Profiles are flown straight (bank 0), as s3.6.1 allows; the bank for the installation effect comes from the
   track, in `n65_doc29`.
   **Found on ANP:** every procedure flies on the reference day, a hot calm day (30 C, QNH 29.80) and a cold windy one
   (-5 C, 20 kt). Departures never run backwards and keep positive thrust. Approaches end on the runway and never climb
   on the reference day. On the hot day, B-59/B-60 lift some Descend-Idle starts 11 to 85 ft above the Level-Idle step
   before them; Doc 29 only says "warn the user", so it is left as written.
   **Gotcha:** a mutation run on a module loaded by `spec_from_file_location` must clear `scripts/__pycache__` and run
   with `-B`. An edit of the same size in the same second otherwise loads the stale .pyc (one variant here "made no
   difference" for that reason).

   (3) s3.6.4/s3.6.5 sub-segmentation (initial climb heights, speed changes over 20 kt) belongs to path construction in

   (4) Volume 3 has no approach case off ISA or off sea level, so the idle adjustments are held only by the made-up
