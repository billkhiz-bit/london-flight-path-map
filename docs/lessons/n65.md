# N65 per postcode (planes louder than 65 dB a day)

Read before touching `scripts/n65_gate.py`, `scripts/n65_profiles.py`, anything under `data/n65/`, or EASA's tables.

## What it is and why (2026-10-15 plan, 27-28 Oct groundwork)

N65 is how many planes a day pass louder than 65 dB LAmax: the CAA's own supplementary metric (ERCD reports, Tables
16-20) and what Battersea's "one plane every five minutes" means. Planes overhead counts what passes INSIDE a cone;
N65 counts what is LOUD, so it also answers the cone's knife edge (Hounslow TW3 3QL: 7 a day in its cell, 339 in the
next), where a postcode just off the path hears planes it is not under. Computed by ECAC Doc 29 **5th edition** (May
2026; step 2) from our tracks and EASA's Aircraft Noise and Performance (ANP) data. The CAA's ANCON 2.4, which makes
the yardstick, follows the 4th (ERCD 2601 s2.2-2.3); its next version follows the 5th.

## THE PUBLISH RULE IS CODE, WRITTEN FIRST (2026-10-28)

`scripts/n65_gate.py` holds Bill's 27 Oct rule ("yes to both") as `publish_verdict(runs, database)`, N65's
`coverage_verdict()`, written before any model so the model is built to a fixed test. Heathrow against ERCD 2601
(annual average 16-hour day, 30 Mar 2025 - 29 Mar 2026): areas above 50/100/200 events within -10%/+15% at each
level, above 500 within +-30%, population inside the 50-event contour within +-20%. Gatwick against ERCD 2602 (summer,
16 Jun - 15 Sep 2025): -10%/+15% at every level (20 to 500). Under-reading gets the tight side. Like for like only
(the CAA's exact period, day and window). The deciding run is on EASA's FULL database; the free v2.3 tables only ever
build. Any miss or anything unmeasured: nothing is published. `tests/test_n65_gate.py` holds every edge both ways and
checks each target against the report's own text (CACI, not ONS, counts the CAA's population: hence +-20%).

### THE FROZEN METHOD (Bill's rulings, 2026-11-12, "go with recommended", on the 12 Nov review's recommendations)

Written BEFORE any 2025 tracks are fetched and before anyone has seen a Gatwick result. A deciding run uses exactly
this; a change after a result is seen voids that run. The one rule above all still stands: never tune to the CAA.

1. **Movements we cannot compute** (no type, no candidate, no track, no air: about 3.1% of a day, plus the plane
   counter's ~1.5% shortfall against the CAA's monthly totals). Each day's counts are SCALED to the CAA's movements for
   that day, the factor reported per day; the unscaled result is reported beside, always. The CAA's movement totals are
   an INPUT of theirs, not their N65 answer. BUILT as: the day's month's CAA Table 03 GRAND TOTAL (the count ERCD uses:
   2025's 479,996 = ERCD 2601's 1,315.0 a day x 365, to rounding) over the month's days, times ERCD 2601's ANNUAL 16-hour
   share (1,232.0 para 3.74 / 1,315.0 para 3.56 = 0.9369), over the movements computed (`n65_day.caa_day_movements()`,
   `--caa-month` required; a day without it is refused). July 2026: 41,206 / 31 x 0.9369 = 1,245.3 a 16-hour day.
   **Bill, 2026-11-12 ("go with recommendation"): the ANNUAL 16-hour share for every day, never each season's own** (they
   differ by 0.26%): the CAA's Heathrow N65 is the annual average 16-hour day, so the share matches its period.
2. **Doc 29's distribution of levels (5th edition, Volume 2 s5.4): ON.** A Gaussian with S = 2 dB, taken from the text
   ("around 2 dB"), never fitted; the table level read as the decibel average and moved to the median by equation
   5-11 (L0 = L - S^2 ln10 / 20, 0.4605 dB at S = 2); events counted fractionally by 5-12
   (`n65_day.exceed_probability()`: a table level of exactly 65 dB counts 0.409 of an event, 63 dB 0.109, 67 dB 0.779).
   Levels are computed down to 55.46 dB for it (a share under 3e-7 below, said). The count without it is reported beside.
3. **The stage.** Both ends of the bracket (lightest and heaviest stage) must pass the gate at every level
   (`n65_gate.bracket_verdict()`). Only a per-flight trip-length source, chosen and ruled before the deciding run, may
   replace the bracket.
4. **Default thrust flown at the observed heights ("the cheaper test")**: an UPPER BOUND only, never the model
   (`n65_day.py day --variant hybrid`, `hybrid_heights()`; the gate runner says a hybrid run decides nothing). The ANP

## The population count (item 3, built 12 Nov 2026, night)

`scripts/n65_population.py` counts the people inside a contour the CAA's way (ERCD 2601 s2.25: each postcode's people
at one point, its centroid), and `n65_gate_run.py` gives each stage end's count to the gate's `population_above_50`.
- **People:** ONS, "Postcode resident and household estimates, England and Wales: Census 2021", P001 (usual residents by
  postcode by sex, Census Day 21 March 2021, postcodes as at May 2021 with at least one usual resident), the two sexes
  summed: https://www.nomisweb.co.uk/output/census/2021/pcd_p001.csv (52.7 MB, 2,754,926 rows, read 12 Nov). Source:
  Office for National Statistics, Crown copyright, Open Government Licence v3.0 (ONS's terms: its content is OGL but
  for third-party images; Nomis's own page names no licence). 1,377,463 postcodes, 59,596,726 people: 816 under TS001's
  59,597,542 for England and Wales (disclosure control moves small counts; the test holds the sum within 0.01%).
- **Place:** NSPL's grid reference (`postcode_grid.grid_ref`, the main clone's August 2026 NSPL, newest postcode 202607):
  every Census postcode placed, none from lat/long, none unplaced.
- **Inside:** the point's cell (flat `j * nx + i` from the grid's corner, as `n65_day.Grid`) reaches the level by the same
  `>=` the areas use; never a bounding box. **Refused:** no population file (the runner, unless `--no-population`, when
  the gate says "not measured" and fails); another Census table, a part-person count, a repeated row; counts for
  another grid; a 50-event contour that reaches the grid's edge (people beyond it would be missing). Gatwick's rule has
  no population check, so its runs never ask for the file.
- **Choices, stated:** a postcode RETIRED since 2021 keeps its people (NSPL keeps its grid reference; 917 postcodes,
  20,301 people nationally); a postcode NEW since May 2021 has no count (its people were counted under the old one), so
  homes built since are missed: ours reads under CACI's 2025 update before any model gap (s2.26: CACI's 2025 database
  counted 2% more than its 2024 one inside the 54 dB contour). LARGE USERS (`usrtypind` 1) keep their residents: the
  Census counts only people living there, so a PO box with nobody living at it has no row; rule A is about planes figures
  at delivery offices, not people the Census found. Inside the July contours they hold 574 to 656 people (0.1-0.2%).
- **The file** (`data/n65/population/residents-2021.npz`, built by `python -I -B scripts/n65_population.py build --census
  data/n65/population/pcd_p001.csv --nspl <main clone>/data/nspl.csv`, and the downloaded `pcd_p001.csv` beside it) exists
